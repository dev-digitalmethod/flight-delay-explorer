From flight records to insights

How Digital Method turned public flight records into an interactive dashboard to compare seasonal patterns and investigate a sudden spike in delays.

Open the Flight Delay Explorer

From public ZIP files to a browser dashboard

We downloaded monthly archives from BTS TranStats, converted the CSV records to typed Parquet, and checked the monthly coverage. DuckDB queried 46.9 million records across 86 months, from July 2019 through August 2026. A compact export lets visitors filter the data and reproduce monthly charts in the browser. To investigate the July spike, we returned to the daily flight records.

As a validation check, the pipeline reproduced the published October 2025 arrival and departure counts and on-time rates for Raleigh-Durham and Charlotte at the report’s published precision.

Seasonality

A January spike tells only part of the story

Raleigh-Durham’s average departure delay was 13.49 minutes in January 2024 and 4.93 minutes in February. Across all reporting carriers from 2022 through 2025, July had the highest average at 20.09 minutes, versus 12.50 in January. The difference illustrates why an airport-specific spike and a national seasonal pattern answer different questions.

Try it
In the Explorer, select RDU, January through December 2024, and Mean. Compare January with February, then look at July and switch Origin airport to All.

Event analysis

Following the July 19, 2024 spike

Reported U.S. cancellations rose from 4.73% on July 18 to 16.53% on July 19. Average departure delay among flights with a recorded delay rose from 29.87 to 71.87 minutes. Delta Air Lines alone recorded 1,325 cancellations on July 19. The timeline aligns with CrowdStrike’s Falcon content update that crashed affected Windows systems. BTS flight data shows the disruption’s timing and scale. CrowdStrike and the U.S. Department of Transportation provide the incident context.
